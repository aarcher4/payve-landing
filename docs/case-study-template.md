# Payve case-study template

Distilled 2026-07-07 from live walks of ramp.com/customers (+ /customers/hingham), happyrobot.ai/customers (+ story pages), and the earlier survey of Mercury and Giga customer proof.

## What the best ones do

**Ramp** (the strongest model)
- Hub: quote-led thematic hero ("Healthier businesses run on Ramp."), then cards. Card anatomy: `metric` + `unit` + plain-language outcome ("&gt;30 hours of monthly AP work saved") stacked over a narrative headline ("The 192-year-old bank that banks on Ramp to take the waste out of its own books") + industry/segment tag.
- Detail page: full-bleed hero using the customer's own real-world photograph with their logo overlaid, narrative H1, then a 3-stat band where each stat carries a plain-language sub ("Accounts payable dropped from a full-time job to less than a couple hours a day"). Big centered pull quote follows. Body ~2,000 words, editorial H2s built on one metaphor carried through, 3+ blockquotes from named people, related stories, closing CTA.

**HappyRobot**
- Hub: company-name cards, one flagship gets the metric-as-headline treatment ("200,000 Calls. Zero Extra Headcount."). Cards link to /customer-story/{name}.

**Giga / Mercury**
- One anchor customer spotlight with a single sharp metric beats many shallow logos (Giga: DoorDash, "0% DWR"). Mercury weaves founder quotes inline instead of a case-study hub.

## The Payve template

Hub (`/customers`): eyebrow + thematic headline, logo/wordmark wall, then story cards. Card = metric line (t-num, display weight) + plain-language outcome + narrative headline + industry tag. No filters until 20+ stories.

Detail (`/customers/{slug}`):
1. Hero: atmosphere image (customer's world: fields, packhouse, border) with wordmark, narrative H1 naming the customer and the change.
2. Stat band: 2-3 stats, each with a plain-language sub. Only defensible numbers; "dozens of hours" is fine when that is the customer's own language.
3. Pull quote from a named person with title.
4. Body 600-1,200 words: the operation → the friction → what Payve runs now → what changed. One metaphor maximum, carried through the H2s.
5. "What they run on Payve" checklist (products used).
6. Related stories + CTA band.

Copy rules (locked lexicon applies in full): dollars only, no rates; no rail vendor names; agents framed as organizational intelligence + approval-gated workflows; no invented metrics — every number traces to the customer's own words, the signed material, or measured platform data. Named customers, quotes, and numbers ship only after customer sign-off (Alex gates before DNS cutover).
